Hacking Biology was introduced to the **[Rapamycin News](https://www.rapamycin.news)** community
— one of the largest and most technically serious longevity forums online — in a
[thread opened by the forum's admin](https://www.rapamycin.news/t/hacking-biology-new-member-developing-a-longevity-biohacking-plan-platform/15776),
who had noticed a new member "getting serious about longevity biohacking and in the process of
developing his plan and a software platform".

That thread is where the project first met the people it was built for.

## What was presented

The presentation shared then is still the clearest statement of the original idea, and it is
worth reading as a historical document — most of it survived into what
[biohack.it](https://biohack.it) is becoming.

<p><a class="btn primary" href="/pdf/hacking-biology-project-presentation-2024.pdf">Read the 2024 presentation (PDF, 14 slides)</a></p>

Its argument, in order:

**The motivation is personal before it is technical.** Slide four says it plainly: *"I am 43; I
know my body is changing with a slow functional loss and I do want to act on it."* The research
already exists but there is no comprehensive approach to applying it — very rich people buy their
way into high-end clinics, and everyone else buys pills on a monthly subscription and hopes.

**The method is measurement, not belief.** Aging shows up as changes at many biological levels,
and most of those levels can be measured. So: measure biomarkers, act, measure again, and judge
the action by what moved. The deck budgets for **130+ biomarkers** — a wide blood panel from
lipids up to NAD+, DNA methylation and telomere length, microbiome, DEXA — collected frequently
enough to produce a trend rather than a snapshot.

**The interventions are treated as a landscape, not a shopping list**, from supplements and
pharmaceuticals through peptides, plasmapheresis and an HBOT protocol with alternating
hyperoxia — each one an experiment with a schedule, not a purchase.

**The hard part is organisation.** Running a serious DIY trial on yourself means scheduling
compounds, booking measurements, keeping cycles straight and recording what actually happened.
That is what the software was proposed to do.

**And the point of the software is other people.** The deck's phrase for it was a *"kind of
crowd-sourced trial"* — knowingly not a scientifically diligent, reproducible protocol, but a way
of generating investigative leads that nobody is currently generating, because everyone's data
dies in a private spreadsheet.

## What was promised to the community

Two commitments were made in that thread, and they are the reason the
[Foundation](/foundation/) exists in the shape it does today:

> most of the people don't do proper measurement and related data management … don't stick to a
> documented protocol of what they are doing, making it difficult or impossible to compare

and, on what the project would be:

> a non-commercial, collaborative and opensource hacker-led biohacking project on longevity

Everything since — the AGPL licence, the open dataset, the non-profit that cannot sell the
software — is that second sentence turned into structure.

The thread has since been read some 2,700 times. The community it addressed is still where this
project's work gets argued with, which is the correct place for it.
