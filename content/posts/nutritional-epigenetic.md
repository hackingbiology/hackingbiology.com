Today i come up with a lovely paper focusing specifically on improving DNA methylation "[Potential reversal of biological age in women following an 8-week methylation-supportive diet and lifestyle program: a case series](https://www.aging-us.com/article/204602/text)" .

It comes up an interesting concept, epinutrition (or nutritional epigenetic), that's the specific set of food, compounds (and i'd add drugs) that specifically focus on improving the changes happening on DNA methylation and histone modifications.

"Epinutrients may be defined as dietary nutrients that provide either substrates or cofactors for DNA methylation activity or influence the expression or rate of activity of DNA methylation-related enzymes. **Folate** and **betaine**, for example, are cofactors in methylation biosynthetic pathways, **alpha ketoglutarate, vitamin C, and vitamin A** are ten-eleven translocation (TET) demethylase cofactors and modulators, and **curcumin**, **epigallocatechin gallate (EGCG)**, **rosmarinic acid, quercetin, and luteolin** are known polyphenolic modulators of DNA methyl transferase (DMNT) enzymes "

With additions to lifestyle changes in terms of nutrition and excercise (30min x 5 times a week), there was an average reduction in just 2 months of -4.6 biological age (with DnaM clock):

Also the paper [Epigenetics: A New Bridge between Nutrition and Health highlight](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S216183132200552X) interesting Epigenetic nutritions agents:

[**Folate**](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/pharmacology-toxicology-and-pharmaceutical-science/folic-acid), **vitamin B-12**, [**methionine**](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/pharmacology-toxicology-and-pharmaceutical-science/methionine), [**choline**](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/pharmacology-toxicology-and-pharmaceutical-science/choline), and **[betaine](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/pharmacology-toxicology-and-pharmaceutical-science/trimethylglycine)**can affect DNA methylation and [histone methylation](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/biochemistry-genetics-and-molecular-biology/histone-methylation) through altering 1-carbon metabolism. Two metabolites of 1-carbon metabolism can affect methylation of DNA and [histones](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/biochemistry-genetics-and-molecular-biology/histone): **[*S*-adenosylmethionine](https://www.nature.com/articles/s42003-022-03280-5) (AdoMet)5**, which is a methyl donor for methylation reactions, and **[*S*-adenosylhomocysteine (AdoHcy)](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/nursing-and-health-professions/s-adenosylhomocysteine)**, which is a product inhibitor of [methyltransferases](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/biochemistry-genetics-and-molecular-biology/methyltransferase). Thus, theoretically, any nutrient, bioactive component, or condition that can affect **AdoMet** or **AdoHcy** levels in the tissue can alter the methylation of DNA and histones. Other water-soluble B vitamins like [**biotin**](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/pharmacology-toxicology-and-pharmaceutical-science/biotin), [**niacin**](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/biochemistry-genetics-and-molecular-biology/niacin), and [**pantothenic acid**](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/pharmacology-toxicology-and-pharmaceutical-science/pantothenic-acid) also play important roles in [histone](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/pharmacology-toxicology-and-pharmaceutical-science/histone) modifications. Biotin is a substrate of histone [biotinylation](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/biochemistry-genetics-and-molecular-biology/biotinylation). Niacin is involved in histone ADP-ribosylation as a substrate of poly(ADP-ribose) [polymerase](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/biochemistry-genetics-and-molecular-biology/polymerase) as well as [histone acetylation](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/biochemistry-genetics-and-molecular-biology/histone-acetylation) as a substrate of [Sirt1](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/biochemistry-genetics-and-molecular-biology/sirtuin-1), which functions as histone deacetylase (HDAC) ([1](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S216183132200552X#bib1)). Pantothenic acid is a part of CoA to form acetyl-CoA, which is the source of [acetyl](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/pharmacology-toxicology-and-pharmaceutical-science/acetic-acid-derivative) group in histone acetylation. Bioactive food components directly affect [enzymes](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/biochemistry-genetics-and-molecular-biology/enzyme) involved in [epigenetic mechanisms](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/biochemistry-genetics-and-molecular-biology/epigenetic-mechanism). For instance, **[genistein](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/biochemistry-genetics-and-molecular-biology/genistein)**and **tea [catechin](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/biochemistry-genetics-and-molecular-biology/catechin)** affects DNA [methyltransferases](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/pharmacology-toxicology-and-pharmaceutical-science/methyltransferase) (Dnmt). [**Resveratrol**](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/pharmacology-toxicology-and-pharmaceutical-science/resveratrol), [**butyrate**](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/pharmacology-toxicology-and-pharmaceutical-science/butyrate), [**sulforaphane**](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/biochemistry-genetics-and-molecular-biology/sulforaphane), and [**diallyl**](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/pharmacology-toxicology-and-pharmaceutical-science/allyl-sulfide) **[sulfide](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/pharmacology-toxicology-and-pharmaceutical-science/allyl-sulfide)**inhibit [HDAC](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/pharmacology-toxicology-and-pharmaceutical-science/histone-deacetylase) and **[curcumin](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/pharmacology-toxicology-and-pharmaceutical-science/curcumin)**inhibits [histone acetyltransferases](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/biochemistry-genetics-and-molecular-biology/histone-acetyltransferase) (HAT).
