Hacking Biology is a **non-profit foundation**. Its job is narrow and permanent: to own
[biohack.it](https://biohack.it) and to guarantee the properties the community has to be able to
trust — for longer than any individual's interest in the project.

## Why two names, one mission

**biohack.it** is the software and the initiative: the open platform people use to document
protocols and measure them. **Hacking Biology** is the non-profit that stewards it — it directs
the project and its development, and exists to *guarantee* what the software promises.

The separation is not cosmetic. A tool can be forked, sold, or captured. A steward with a mission
lock is what makes the promise credible and permanent. The Foundation is the guarantor;
biohack.it is the thing guaranteed.

## Ownership

The Foundation **owns the biohack.it software, brand, trademark and copyright**. It cannot
relicense the software away from **AGPL-3.0**, and it cannot relicense it away from the mission.

The Foundation is to be incorporated in **Roatán, Honduras, within the Próspera ZEDE** — a special
economic zone with regulatory autonomy — chosen deliberately to preserve a progressive spirit of
experimentation in longevity and biohacking, alongside an aligned community.

Incorporation is not data hosting. Where the entity is registered is a separate question from
where user data lives: for users in regulated jurisdictions such as the EU, data-protection duties
are met at the **hosting and technical layer**, independently of the Próspera seat.

## What the Foundation guarantees

**Never commercial.** The project is never sold, taken proprietary, or turned into a walled
garden. This is the reason a foundation holds it, and not a company.

**Open by code and by data.** Software under AGPL-3.0. The public dataset released as a clonable
snapshot. Content under open licences. Anyone can run their own instance — including against the
Foundation, if it ever fails at this.

**Harm reduction first.** Safety guardrails are not optional features. The platform *describes,
never prescribes*; the reason it exists is that beginners already copy off-label protocols
without monitoring, and they deserve better than a forum thread.

**Transparency of method.** Where AI is used and where it is not is published. Every upstream
project whose work is reused is attributed and notified: this project is a connector of other
people's effort, not a silent consumer of it.

**Data belongs to the person.** Full export, always. The individual owns the record, not the
platform.

## Governance

The Foundation holds the stewardship: the domain, the trademark, the software copyright, the
canonical instance, and the guarantee of everything above.

**The board is a basket of biohackers and software hackers** — practitioners of the domain and
builders of the tool — composed so the project stays impartial and on-mission over time. Mixing
the two makes both capture and drift harder: the biohackers hold it to the community's needs, the
hackers hold it to open, verifiable engineering.

Development is directed by the Foundation with an open contributor community; architecture
decisions and specifications are public from day one. Community governance attaches to the
community that already exists rather than to a cold-launched forum, and contested claims are
discussed in the open rather than triaged privately.

## Sustainability

The Foundation is funded **by public grants**. Beyond grants, equity participations in dedicated
startups pursuing the *same* goal may arise — aligned ventures whose success feeds the mission,
never the reverse.

The knowledge is never for sale. Protocols, biomarkers, safety rules and the open dataset are not
products, and nothing in the funding model lets a supplier buy influence over which protocols
emerge.

## What could go wrong

Stating the failure modes is part of being accountable for them.

| Risk | What holds it back |
|---|---|
| Mission drift over time | Charter, AGPL, and open-data licences that cannot be quietly reversed; mixed biohacker/hacker board |
| Funding pressure toward commercialisation | Public-grant funding; only mission-aligned equity; knowledge never sold |
| Capture of the canonical instance | Full clonability and self-hosting — the community can always leave and take the data |
| Regulatory qualification as a medical device | Descriptive-not-prescriptive posture by design; legal review before public launch |
| Jurisdiction versus data protection | Próspera seat for the entity; user data protection handled at the hosting layer |

## Two sites, two jobs

[biohack.it](https://biohack.it) is the **product** site: what the platform is, the plan, how to
join the community. It speaks to biohackers.

This site is the **organisation**: who the Foundation is, what it guarantees, how it is governed,
and how to support or partner with it. It speaks to institutions, researchers, funders, and the
sceptical clinician.

One message from both — *open infrastructure for longevity, stewarded so it stays that way.*
